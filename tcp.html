True Capacity

An honest look at load vs. capacity — before the plan, not after the miss.

This is not a productivity score. It's a look at how much value-delivering work your team can realistically produce in a given window — and, more importantly, how much capacity is hiding in plain sight, waiting to be reclaimed. Every number below is yours to change. The point isn't the floor. It's the climb — and the line you shouldn't cross to reach it.

Extraneous load — concurrent priorities & interruptions per person3
The reclaimable kind of cognitive load: context-switching and interruptions imposed by how work is organized. 1 = fully focused. (The two kinds you can't reclaim are in the next step.)

2bCognitive load — the nature of the work

Two kinds of load come from the work itself, not the worker: intrinsic (how inherently complex the task is) and germane (how much new learning it demands). Both lower finished output per hour, both are outside any individual's control — and unlike the extraneous load above, you cannot reclaim them by fixing process. They're the floor the work sets.

3How alive is the work

The hours that remain aren't all equal. An engaged person and a checked-out one deliver very different value in the same hour. This is the human multiplier pure planning ignores. Defaults are Gallup's global reality — change them to your team.

4What you've committed

How much of the raw, on-paper capacity has this team already been committed to deliver this window? Most plans commit 85–100% of the paper number — and then reality adds more after the plan is "fixed."

The shoulder-tap is the manager who walks past the plan to their favorite developer with an off-sprint "quick favor." It's the expediter position in an Agile sprint — it injects scope the plan never held and forces context-switching, so it costs more than its raw hours. And why work still feels slow — the flow lens

Capacity is how much your people can do. Flow is whether the work is moving. These are different problems — a busy team and a stalled one look identical from a status deck.
